# Session 10 - Decent into the Mines
## Into Sector X

The party resumed just inside the breach into **Sector X**, accompanied by all three chuul. Beyond the mine wall lay something clearly ancient and deliberately constructed: a vast, dark chamber dominated by a crumbling **spiral staircase descending around an apparently bottomless central shaft**. Faded carvings and murals covered the inner wall, while faint points of residuum provided the only illumination.

The chuul were unusually calm—almost intrigued by the place. Noranor asked Bastrix what it was. The response was cryptic:

> **Bastrix:** “We remember. Not a place.”

Whatever that meant, the chuul seemed to recognize something about the space.

The stairs immediately proved dangerous. Noranor slipped badly and ended up dangling over the abyss, but Koltoc and Tieg managed to haul them back up before they fell. The party continued deeper into the spiral.

## The Walls Are Watching

Algvirek stopped to cast **Comprehend Languages** so he could decipher the enormous murals. While studying them, he noticed something stranger: thin cracks in the ancient stone contained residuum-like light that **pulsed in response to the party’s movement**.

Koltoc proposed an experiment and asked everyone—including the chuul—to hold perfectly still.

The lights quieted.

Then one chuul shifted, and a brilliant pulse raced through the cracks like a vast organic web or branching network.

Koltoc reached an uncomfortable conclusion:

> **Koltoc:** “Yep. It knows we’re here.”

But what exactly was reacting to them? The stone? The residuum? Something built into the structure? The party had no clear answer.

## Reading the First Murals

Because the first mural stretched dozens of feet upward, Noranor used Wild Shape to become a **giant eagle** and carried Algvirek into the cavern so he could get a better view.

From a distance, the mural became clearer. One side depicted rigid order: ranks of robed figures, geometric cities, and strange stars. The other showed warriors, weapons, mounts, smoke, and chaos. Between them stood two figures whose hands nearly touched.

Algvirek deciphered their names:

**Fenevir and Xamodas the Bold.**

The party began to realize that the murals were not simply decorative. They appeared to be recording some kind of history.

## Algvirek Sends the Owl Down

Wanting to determine how deep the shaft continued, Algvirek summoned a great horned owl familiar and sent it directly downward rather than following the staircase.

At roughly seventy feet, the experiment went badly.

Through the familiar, Algvirek began hearing **countless voices**: familiar languages, unknown languages, animals, waves, heartbeat-like rhythms, and forms of communication that should not have made sense at all.

The psychic overload caused real physical harm. Blood appeared at Algvirek’s eyes and ears, and he was overwhelmed by simultaneous grief and ecstatic joy.

When he tried to order the owl back, something seemed to interfere. His connection stretched painfully as the familiar continued downward while the party hurried down the spiral stairs to close the distance.

Eventually Algvirek regained control. The battered owl returned, bloodied and traumatized, and he immediately dismissed it.

Whatever lay deeper in the shaft, the owl’s descent had exposed Algvirek to something the party did not yet understand.

## A History Written Down the Spiral

The party continued descending, stopping periodically to examine murals.

One showed two armed factions laying down their weapons beneath a central figure whose chains wound through grotesquely intertwined bodies. Two figures walked away together beneath the words:

**“Born from stars and painted with blood.”**

Another showed Fenevir and Xamodas beside broken weapons and a shared cup, with a damaged fragment beginning:

**“To fight…”**

Then the artistic style changed completely.

Water curled around two apparently dead figures beneath an immense, gentle face, carrying and restoring them. Spirals appeared everywhere—in water, clouds, hair, and robes—and a strange chariot emerged from the sea.

The inscription read:

**“Eternity stretches farther out than your lives will ever see. Find your peace far out at sea.”**

Was the figure a person? A deity? A symbolic representation? The mural offered no clear explanation.

## The Journey Between Worlds

The next murals followed the chariot through impossible landscapes: alien stars, strange worlds, an upside-down ocean, unfamiliar planets, and distant moons.

Across each realm, glowing lines converged on the same recurring symbol:

**the spiral.**

It appeared as a galaxy, a storm, a shell, a whirlpool, and finally a gigantic eye.

Was the repetition symbolic? A map? A religious image? A warning? The party could only speculate.

The chariot eventually descended toward the archipelago. Beneath it was written:

**“Three times around, they drove their chariot. Now we are free and loved, the children of safety.”**

The phrase connected to something the party had already learned: **Mirtalyanki** meant *Children of Safety*.

The murals appeared to be telling a much older story about the origins of the island people, although how literal that story was remained uncertain.

## Dreams That Aren’t Yours

Another mural showed Fenevir and Xamodas resting above branching vein-like structures connecting the islands beneath them.

Later images showed them clutching their heads, their thoughts represented by tangled and jagged lines.

Algvirek translated the inscription:

**“On the first night, he dreamed of an ocean that never existed. On the second, she remembered a sky she had never seen. On the third, they dreamt a dream that was not theirs.”**

Koltoc immediately recognized the experience.

> **Koltoc:** “That’s what I saw.”

The words resembled Koltoc’s own strange dreams following exposure to residuum.

Whether that meant the experiences were connected—or merely similar—was still unclear.

## The Eye

The murals became increasingly disturbing.

One depicted an **enormous eye connected to an intricate branching structure**, stretching across seas and landmasses on an almost incomprehensible scale. Tiny figures around it screamed, collapsed, or clawed at themselves.

The inscription read:

**“There are things so old that to remember them is to give into madness.”**

The image raised more questions than it answered.

Was the eye literal? Symbolic? A representation of memory, perception, or something else entirely? And why had the artists chosen to place it alongside scenes of terror and madness?

The party could not know.

## The Chuul

The next mural changed tone dramatically.

It showed people riding chuul, befriending them, petting them, and resting beside them. Unlike the tangled imagery of the previous murals, the people here were surrounded by smooth, peaceful lines.

One central image showed a person kneeling beside a chuul, the two leaning their faces together while a spiral radiated outward.

The party had already noticed that the chuul behaved differently in this place, and the mural suggested that the creatures had some special significance to the people who once lived here.

But what exactly was that relationship? Companionship? Ritual? Protection? Something else?

The murals did not say.

## The Five Principles

Near the bottom of the spiral, the final mural presented five teachings:

**Isolation is protection.**
**Inaction is power.**
**Peace, not war.**
**Water is life.**
**Love is all.**

The final image showed generations of people surrounding two figures whose foreheads touched, with chuul gathered beneath them.

Below everything was an **enormous closed eye**.

By the end of the descent, the murals had revealed fragments of a story: conflict, escape, travel, dreams, fear, community, and a set of principles that seemed to shape the people who built this place.

But major questions remained.

What was the eye?
Why did the spiral appear across so many different images?
Why did the walls react to movement?
What had happened to Fenevir and Xamodas?
Why were the chuul so important?
And why did Koltoc’s own experiences resemble what was depicted on the walls?

At last, the staircase ended.

The party reached rubble-strewn ground beside a roughly **twenty-foot-high cavern entrance** leading farther into the ruins. Residuum glowed more strongly here, and beyond the opening lay only darkness.

The session ended with the party standing before the cavern mouth, with far more questions than answers.
